// 01 — about

Hardware built for manufacturing, not just for a demo.

I design production-oriented PCBs in KiCad, covering the full path from schematic capture through component placement, routing, and 4-layer stack-up planning, to Gerbers, pick-and-place files and BOMs a contract manufacturer can actually build from.

During a 6-month PCB design internship I worked on real design, testing and documentation tasks — not just tutorials — which is where the DFM/DFA habits below came from. I'm now looking for a PCB Design Engineer or Hardware Engineer role where I can keep building boards people rely on.

Alongside layout work, I'm growing my embedded firmware skills on ESP32, — because a hardware engineer who understands the firmware side designs better boards.

// 04 — how I work

From schematic to shipped board

The same six-stage process on every project, regardless of size.

01Schematic

Capture circuit intent, verify against datasheets.

02PCB Layout

Placement, routing, power & ground planning.

034-Layer Design

Stack-up, impedance and signal integrity checks.

04DFM Review

Design rule checks against fab constraints.

05Manufacturing

Gerbers, BOM, pick & place, panelization.

06Hardware Testing

Bring-up, validation, documentation.
